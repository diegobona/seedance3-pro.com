Seedance 2.5 Image-to-Video Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-17

For image-to-video, the still image defines what the scene looks like; the prompt describes how it moves. This guide walks through the workflow, prompt structure and settings, with examples and ways to fix common problems.

What changed

Seedance 2.5 supports longer clips and more control

ByteDance says Seedance 2.5 can generate up to 30 seconds in one pass, use up to 30 images, 10 videos and 10 audio clips as references, and edit at specific timestamps. Longer clips and more references call for closer planning of which assets to use and when each action happens.

How to use Seedance 2.5 for image-to-video

1. Choose a clear reference image

The source image sets the subject’s identity, composition, lighting, clothing and object shapes. Choose one where the subject is clear, the anatomy looks natural and there is room for the planned motion. Check for stray text and visual artifacts too. A distorted face or product in the input will usually look worse once it moves.

  • Use a sharp image with the subject separated from the background.
  • Match the source aspect ratio to the final platform whenever possible.
  • Leave visual space in the direction the subject or camera will move.
  • Avoid turning a tightly framed portrait into a distant full-body shot in a single step.

2. Define one visual objective

Before writing the prompt, decide what you need from the clip: a moving portrait, a product reveal, a view of a location, a specific camera move or a short story moment. A clip can include several shots, as long as they serve that same goal.

3. Describe how the image should move

The model can see the reference image, so use the prompt to explain what changes: where the eyes look, how the hands and fabric move, the camera path, movement in the environment, sound and the final pose. Use concrete verbs and put the actions in order.

4. Add camera direction and timing

Choose one main camera movement, such as a slow dolly in, a lateral tracking shot, a controlled orbit, a tilt up or a locked-off shot. For longer clips, give each action a time range. Timestamps help keep story moments separate and reduce conflicting motion instructions.

5. Add consistency constraints

List the details that must stay the same: facial features, body proportions, hairstyle, clothing details, product logo placement, background layout and lighting direction. Keep these constraints short and specific.

6. Generate short, evaluate, and extend

Start with a short draft to check the subject’s identity and movement. Look at the first and last frames, hands, face, camera stability, background geometry and audio synchronization. Fix the biggest problem first. Once the motion works, generate a longer version or extend the sequence.

Seedance 2.5 prompt formula

You can build an image-to-video prompt in this order:

[Subject anchor] + [primary action] + [camera movement] + [timing] + [environmental response] + [visual style] + [audio direction] + [consistency constraints]

Example:

Use @Image 1 as the exact visual reference. The woman slowly turns toward the window and lifts the curtain with her right hand. The camera makes a subtle dolly in from a medium shot to a medium close-up over 8 seconds. Morning light grows warmer across her face while the curtain and loose hair move gently in the breeze. Natural cinematic realism, restrained motion, quiet room ambience. Preserve her facial features, red dress, body proportions, balcony geometry, and lighting direction. No sudden cuts, no extra fingers, no text.

Prompt examples

Portrait with subtle natural motion

Use @Image 1 as the identity and composition reference. The subject blinks naturally, takes a slow breath, and turns her eyes toward the light. Locked camera with an almost imperceptible push in. Soft hair movement from a gentle breeze, realistic skin texture, shallow depth of field. Preserve facial identity, hairstyle, earrings, clothing, and background. No talking, no exaggerated expression, no camera shake.

Product reveal

Use @Image 1 as the exact product reference. Begin with the bottle in silhouette, then move a narrow highlight from left to right across the glass as the camera performs a smooth 20-degree orbit. Fine condensation forms on the surface and the background mist drifts slowly. Premium studio advertising, crisp reflections, controlled black-and-lime palette. Keep the bottle shape, cap, label, typography, and logo unchanged.

Cinematic scene with timed beats

Use @Image 1 for the character and location. 0–4s: medium shot, the character looks across the empty station as paper moves along the platform. 4–8s: the camera tracks laterally while the character begins walking. 8–12s: a train light appears in the distance and the camera slowly pulls back to a wide shot. Moody cinematic realism, cool night lighting, distant rail ambience. Preserve face, coat, station layout, and screen direction across the sequence.

Motion-reference workflow

Use @Image 1 for the subject's exact appearance and @Video 1 only for camera path, pacing, and body movement. Recreate the same controlled arc and final framing while keeping the character, wardrobe, location, and color palette from @Image 1. Do not copy the identity, background, or styling from @Video 1.

How to control references without creating conflicts

Give each reference a clear role and name it in the prompt. For example, one image can define the character’s identity, another their clothing, a video the motion, and an audio clip the rhythm. Adding more files will not necessarily improve the result. If references disagree on the same feature, say which one takes priority.

Seedance 2.5’s official documentation describes stronger control over image, video, audio, motion, creative, clay-render and editing references. This lets you specify which property the model should take from each file, which matters more in practice than filling every available reference slot.

Timestamp editing and extensions

For a longer clip, sketch out the shot schedule before writing the full prompt. Give each time interval one action and one camera instruction. If one section fails, ask for a change to that time range while keeping the rest. When extending a clip, restate the character, environment, visual style, motion direction and sound that need to continue.

Edit @Video 1. Keep the character, wardrobe, setting, lighting, and audio unchanged. Adjust only 6–9s: replace the sudden camera turn with a slow stabilized pan to the right, ending on the same final composition.

Troubleshooting

The face or product changes

Try a smaller pose change, a clearer reference and a shorter first draft. Repeat the visual details that must stay fixed. For a product, describe its shape, material, label position, logo and cap separately.

Motion looks stiff

Replace abstract verbs such as “moves beautifully” with observable movement: shifts weight to the left foot, turns the shoulders, raises the right hand, then looks toward camera. Add secondary motion only after the main action works.

The camera jumps or ignores direction

Remove competing moves and describe one continuous path with a start frame and end frame. “Slow dolly in from medium shot to close-up” is more controllable than a list containing zoom, orbit, pan, handheld shake, and crane movement.

The background warps

Keep the movement shorter and avoid asking a flat source image to produce extreme parallax. Tell the model to preserve buildings, the horizon, straight lines and the spatial layout. A wider source image usually leaves more room for the camera to move.

The clip is too busy or hard to follow

Use fewer subjects, actions, scene changes and style modifiers. Split the story into separate moments; get each one working before extending or editing the sequence.

Seedance 2.5 image-to-video checklist

  • The reference image is sharp and matches the intended framing.
  • The prompt contains one primary action and one primary camera movement.
  • Every reference asset has one clearly defined role.
  • Time ranges do not contain conflicting actions.
  • Identity, wardrobe, object, and background constraints are explicit.
  • The first draft is short enough to diagnose quickly.
  • Only one variable changes between test generations.
  • The final review checks the first frame, last frame, hands, face, geometry, motion, and sound.

Frequently asked questions

Can Seedance 2.5 turn one image into a video?

Yes. Use the image to define the scene, then describe the subject’s motion, camera path, how the environment should respond, duration and details that need to stay consistent.

How long can a Seedance 2.5 video be?

The official Seedance 2.5 page lists up to 30 seconds in a single generation, with extension support. Short drafts remain the fastest way to validate a new idea.

What is the best Seedance 2.5 prompt structure?

Describe the subject first, followed by the action, camera movement and timing. Then add the environment’s response, visual style, audio direction and details that must remain consistent.

Should I use all available references?

Use the references you need to control a specific property. Too many references covering the same details can leave their priority unclear.
